Before you switch, check whose name is on your domain

Your domain is your web address, like yourbusiness.com. ICANN says the registrant, the person or business listed for it, holds the rights to it. If someone else registered it for you, that may not be you, even if you paid. Here's how to check yours, and how we set it up.

What ICANN says

ICANN, the group behind domain names, answers this exact question on its FAQ. If you hired a developer to help manage your domain:

"…they may have registered the domain name using their own contact details (even if you have paid them to register and manage the domain name)."

Their advice:

"Check with your registrar to see who the Registrant of record is for your domain name."

How to check yours with your registrar

  1. Find your registrar. That's the company your domain renews with. Look for a renewal email or invoice, or ask whoever built your site.
  2. Ask who the registrant is. Log in to your account with that registrar, or call them, and ask whose name and email are listed as the registrant. Ask the registrar directly, as ICANN advises.
  3. If it isn't you, ask for it to be changed. Start with whoever registered it. ICANN notes you may need to show proof of your payment to the developer to prove to your registrar that you're the rightful holder, so keep your invoices.

Questions about domains

Do I own my domain?

Not the way you'd own a truck. ICANN says you do not "own" a domain name: paying to register one is "more like leasing" it, and you keep it by renewing before it expires. What matters is that the registrant, the person or business listed, is you.

My web company registered it for me. Is that a problem?

Not always. The question is whose name is listed. If it's theirs, ask them to change the registrant to you, and keep proof that you paid.
