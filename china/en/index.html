01Why it matters

Buyers look at your website before they contact you

Before sending an inquiry, American buyers often look a supplier up online first. They usually have three questions:

  1. 1

    What do you make?

    Who the company is, what it makes or sells, and where the factory or warehouse is.

  2. 2

    Can you deliver?

    Capabilities, processes, specifications and quality control, stated as specific facts.

  3. 3

    How do I reach you?

    What a quote request needs, and how to reach someone who can answer.

A clear, professional English website makes those three answers easier to find.

04Writing in English

The same message, written for American buyers

Many supplier websites use English that buyers can read but can't learn much from. A made-up example:

Common wording

We are professional manufacturer with high quality and best price, welcome to inquiry!

→

Clearer wording

We machine aluminum and steel parts to your drawings. Send us your drawing and quantity, and our sales team will reply with a quote.

WhySaying what you make and what the buyer should do next is more useful than 'professional', 'high quality' or 'best price'.

05Straight talk

What a website can and can't do

Results depend on your products, pricing, service, market conditions and many things outside the website. We don't promise them.

A website can

  • Make it easier for American buyers to understand your company, products and capabilities
  • Give buyers a familiar, professional way to evaluate you
  • Make it easier for buyers to contact you

A website can't guarantee

  • Inquiries, orders or sales
  • Search rankings
  • That buyers will trust you
