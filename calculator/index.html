How the math works

  1. 01

    Customers who slip away each week

    Calls you couldn't pick up, people who never heard back, a form that never reached you. Only count new customers who never came back, not repeat customers or sales calls.

  2. 02

    How many would have hired you

    Out of 10 real calls, how many usually turn into a job? That's your close rate. If you don't know, start low.

  3. 03

    Your average job

    A typical invoice for one customer. Use a normal job, not your biggest one.

  4. 04

    Times 50 working weeks

    Customers a week × close rate × average job × 50 weeks. That's the yearly figure. A month is the year divided by 12.

What a website can and can't do about it

It can

Put a call button on every screen, catch a request with a short form when you can't pick up, and send that request to your email straight away.

It can

Show up when people look you up, on Google, on your Google Business Profile and in AI search tools that can read your site.

It can't

Answer your phone, make a customer hire you or promise a number of calls. Anyone who promises that is guessing.

Questions about the calculator

Is this what I'm losing right now?

No. It's what those customers add up to if your numbers are right. Some of them would never have hired you anyway, and that's why the close rate is in the math.

Does anything I type get saved or sent?

No. The math runs in your browser. Nothing is sent to us or stored.

Why 50 weeks?

It leaves two weeks for holidays and slow weeks. If you work fewer weeks a year, the real figure is lower.
