How to Get More Customers Online: A Practical Guide for Small Businesses

Most new customers now find a business the same way: they search, compare a few options in a minute or two, and contact the one that looks easiest to trust. This guide walks through each step of that path and what to fix at each one, in the order that usually brings results fastest.

Start with how customers actually choose

Before spending money on ads or posting every day, look at the path a customer takes. For most businesses it has four steps:

  1. They search. On Google, on Google Maps, or more and more often by asking an AI assistant such as ChatGPT or Gemini.
  2. They compare. They look at two or three results: reviews, photos, hours, whether the business serves their area.
  3. They check you out. They tap through to your website or Google profile to see if you look current and professional.
  4. They contact someone. They call, text, book or fill in a form, usually with the first business that makes it easy.

Getting more customers online means removing the leaks at each of those steps. A business that shows up but looks outdated loses people at step three. A business with a great site that nobody finds loses them at step one.

1. Make sure you show up on Google and Google Maps

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a customer sees, before your website. It is free, and it is the single biggest lever for local visibility.

  • Claim and verify your profile at business.google.com if you have not already.
  • Pick the right primary category. It is the strongest signal Google uses to decide which searches you appear for. Add secondary categories only if they truly fit.
  • Set accurate hours, phone, website and service area. Wrong hours are one of the fastest ways to lose a customer's trust.
  • Add real photos of your work, your team and your location, and keep adding new ones every month.
  • List your services with short, plain descriptions.

Then search for your own service and town in a private browser window. If you do not appear, or competitors with fewer reviews appear above you, your profile and website are not yet giving Google enough clear information.

2. Build pages around what people search for

Search engine optimization (SEO) is simply making it easy for Google to understand what you do, where you do it and why you are a good answer. The most effective step for most small businesses is having one clear page for each main service, and for the main areas you serve.

  • Use the words customers use. "Water heater replacement" rather than "hydronic solutions". "Mobile car detailing" rather than "automotive appearance services".
  • Put the service and the place in the page title and main heading, for example "Mobile Car Detailing in Crystal Lake, IL".
  • Answer the questions people ask before they buy: price range or how quotes work, how long it takes, what is included, what areas you cover.
  • Make the site fast on a phone. Most local searches happen on phones, and slow pages send people back to the results.
  • Add structured data (schema markup) so search engines can read your business name, services, hours and reviews reliably.

3. Get recommended by AI search tools

A growing share of people ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews questions like "who is a good detailer near me" or "how do I find a reliable roofer". Being named in those answers is often called generative engine optimization (GEO), or AI search optimization.

AI tools build their answers from information they can find and trust. You improve your chances by:

  • Keeping your business facts identical everywhere: name, address or service area, phone, hours and services on your website, Google profile and directory listings.
  • Publishing clear, factual pages that state what you do, who you serve and where, in plain sentences an AI tool can quote.
  • Adding question-and-answer content that matches how people ask, for example "How much does a full interior detail cost?"
  • Earning mentions and reviews on reputable sites in your industry and area.

The work overlaps heavily with SEO, which is why it pays to do both together.

4. Turn visitors into calls, texts and bookings

Plenty of businesses get traffic but few enquiries, because the next step is not obvious. Check your own site on a phone and fix anything that slows a customer down:

  • A tap-to-call and tap-to-text button visible without scrolling.
  • A short contact form: name, phone or email, and one question about the job. Every extra field loses people.
  • Online booking if your work suits it.
  • Your service area and hours stated clearly near the top.
  • Proof: real photos of your work and recent reviews on the page.

Make sure every enquiry reaches you immediately, by text or email. A lead answered in five minutes is far more likely to become a customer than one answered the next day.

5. Earn reviews steadily

Reviews influence both whether you appear and whether people choose you. The businesses that win usually do not have a review campaign once a year; they ask every happy customer, every time.

  • Send a direct link to your Google review form by text right after the job.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad, briefly and politely.
  • Never pay for reviews or only ask customers you know are happy through a filter. Both break Google's rules.

6. Use ads and social media to add to it, not replace it

Paid ads and social media can bring customers quickly, but they work much better when the basics above are in place. An ad that sends people to a slow site with no clear way to call wastes money. Once your profile, site and enquiry path are solid, a small, focused ad budget and regular posts showing your real work can add on top.

Checklist

  • Google Business Profile claimed, verified and fully filled in
  • Correct primary category, hours, phone, website and service area
  • A page for each main service, written in customers' words
  • Service and town in page titles and headings
  • Site loads quickly and reads well on a phone
  • Business facts identical on your site, Google profile and listings
  • Question-and-answer content for what customers ask
  • Tap-to-call, tap-to-text and a short form on every page
  • Every enquiry sent to you instantly
  • A review request sent after every job

Questions

How long does it take to get more customers from Google?

Fixes to your Google Business Profile and website can show results within weeks for local searches. Ranking for broader, competitive searches usually takes several months of steady work.

Do I need a new website to get more customers online?

Not always. Many businesses get more from fixing their Google profile, adding clear service pages and making it easier to call or book than from a full rebuild.

Is SEO or paid advertising better for a small business?

They do different jobs. SEO and a strong Google profile build visibility that keeps working; ads buy attention while you pay. Most small businesses should get the basics right first, then add ads.

What is GEO or AI search optimization?

It is making sure AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews can find accurate information about your business and recommend it when people ask for your type of service.
